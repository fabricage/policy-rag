# Meridian Software Inc. Data Privacy Policy

## Purpose and Scope

This Data Privacy Policy establishes how Meridian Software Inc. classifies, protects, retains, and responds to requests regarding personal data and other business data under its control. The policy applies to employees, contractors, and systems that create, process, store, transmit, or dispose of Meridian Software Inc. data, including customer data and employee data. It is intended to work alongside security, acceptable-use, and records-management standards.

Meridian Software Inc. headquarters timezone for operational deadlines in this policy is Pacific Time, unless a legal deadline is defined by statute or contract in another manner. Questions may be directed to the Data Protection Officer and, for security incidents, to the Security Team through approved channels, including contacts at @meridian-software.example addresses published internally.

## Policy Owner

This policy is owned by the Data Protection Officer (DPO), working with the Chief Information Security Officer (CISO). The DPO leads privacy interpretation, data subject request processes, and privacy training expectations. The CISO leads technical security controls, logging standards for Restricted data access, and incident response alignment. Data owners in business units are responsible for classification decisions within their domains and for quarterly access reviews where required.

## Data Classification Levels

Meridian Software Inc. uses exactly four data classification levels:

1. Public  
2. Internal  
3. Confidential  
4. Restricted  

Public data is approved for general distribution outside the company, such as published marketing pages and public blog posts. Internal data is intended for workforce use and is not for public release, such as ordinary internal process documentation. Confidential data requires controlled access because unauthorized disclosure could harm customers, employees, or Meridian Software Inc. Restricted data is the most sensitive category and requires heightened controls, including access logging and stricter handling limits.

Employees must classify data at creation or receipt whenever practical, and must handle data according to its classification even if a label is missing but the sensitivity is clear from context. When unsure between two levels, employees should choose the more protective classification and consult the DPO or the relevant data owner.

## Customer Data Classification Default

Customer data is classified as Confidential by default unless a contract says Restricted. This default ensures that customer information is not treated as Internal or Public merely because it appears in ordinary support or implementation workflows. Where a customer contract, data processing agreement, or regulatory requirement designates a higher sensitivity, Meridian Software Inc. treats the relevant customer data as Restricted and applies Restricted-level controls.

Teams that onboard new customers should confirm contractual classification requirements during implementation and reflect them in system configuration and access design. Support and engineering staff must not downgrade customer data classification without DPO or contractual authority.

## Access Control and Least Privilege

Access to Internal, Confidential, and Restricted data must be limited to individuals with a legitimate business need. Managers and system owners should grant least-privilege access and remove access promptly when roles change. Credentials and access tokens are personal to the assigned user and must not be shared.

Restricted data access must be logged, and access reviews must be performed quarterly by the data owner. Logging should capture sufficient detail to support investigation and compliance needs, in accordance with standards set with the CISO. Quarterly access reviews must verify that each identity still requires Restricted access, and excess access must be revoked. Data owners should retain evidence of quarterly reviews for audit purposes.

## Handling Rules by Classification

Public data may be shared externally without special approval when it is the current approved public version. Internal data may be shared with workforce members and authorized contractors but should not be posted publicly. Confidential data may be shared only with authorized persons and through approved systems, with encryption in transit according to security standards. Restricted data requires the strictest handling, including logged access, need-to-know limitations, and prohibition on use in unsanctioned tools.

Customer production data may not be copied to personal devices or unsanctioned cloud tools. This rule applies regardless of urgency and includes screenshots, exports, database dumps, and local replicas. If production-like data is needed for testing, teams must use approved sanitized datasets or sanctioned environments that meet Meridian Software Inc. security requirements. Violations of the production-data handling rule are serious and may result in discipline under company conduct policies.

## Retention of Customer Data

Customer data is retained for seven (7) years after contract end unless a shorter period is required by contract or law. Retention clocks and repositories should be designed so that expiration can be executed in a controlled manner. Sales, Customer Success, Legal, and Engineering must not maintain indefinite personal shadow copies that bypass retention schedules.

When a contract requires a shorter retention period, that shorter period controls for the covered data. When law requires a longer or shorter period for specific record types, Meridian Software Inc. will follow the legal requirement for those records. The DPO and Legal should be consulted when retention obligations appear to conflict.

## Retention of Employee Personnel Records

Employee personnel records are retained for seven (7) years after termination. This retention supports legal, tax, and administrative needs while defining an end point for ordinary retention. People Operations is the primary records owner for personnel files, and access to personnel records is limited to authorized roles.

Managers should not keep long-term personal archives of employee sensitive data outside HRIS and approved systems. At the end of the seven-year post-termination retention period, disposal must follow secure destruction standards approved with the CISO and People Operations.

## Privacy by Design and Data Minimization

Product and internal system initiatives at Meridian Software Inc. should incorporate privacy by design. Teams should collect only the personal data needed for stated purposes, define retention at design time, and avoid converting optional analytics into covert profiling. Privacy reviews may be required for high-risk processing, including large-scale monitoring, sensitive personal data, or new cross-border transfers.

Engineers and analysts should prefer aggregated or de-identified data when individual-level data is unnecessary. De-identification does not automatically make data Public; re-identification risk may keep data Confidential or Restricted.

## Data Subject and Privacy Requests

Meridian Software Inc. acknowledges data subject and privacy requests within five (5) business days and fulfills them within thirty (30) calendar days where legally required. Acknowledgment should confirm receipt and, where possible, the type of request received. Fulfillment includes verifying requester identity to an appropriate degree, locating relevant data, applying exemptions allowed by law, and delivering a response through approved channels.

Employees who receive a privacy request directly from a customer, employee, or third party should not attempt to fulfill it alone. They should route the request to the DPO or privacy intake process immediately so that the five-business-day acknowledgment target can be met. The thirty-calendar-day fulfillment target applies where law requires fulfillment on that timeline; where law allows a longer period or a different process, the DPO will direct the response strategy.

## Breach Notification Internal Timeline

If a suspected or confirmed personal data breach or security incident involving personal data is discovered, the Security Team must be notified within four (4) hours of discovery, aligning with the security incident timeline. Notification should include what was discovered, when it was discovered, systems or data types potentially affected, and any immediate containment steps already taken.

Employees must not delay internal notification while they investigate independently for long periods. Containment and escalation can occur in parallel. The CISO and DPO coordinate on regulatory and customer notification decisions after initial triage. Failure to notify the Security Team within four hours of discovery may itself be a policy violation, particularly where delay increases harm.

## Third Parties and Vendors

Vendors that process Meridian Software Inc. or customer personal data must be subject to appropriate contractual protections and security assessments proportionate to data sensitivity. Employees must not place Confidential or Restricted data into a new vendor tool without approved procurement and security review. Vendor access to Restricted data remains subject to logging and quarterly access review expectations where Meridian Software Inc. remains responsible for oversight.

Upon vendor offboarding, access must be removed and return or destruction of data confirmed according to contract. Shadow IT creates privacy and security risk and is prohibited for company or customer data processing.

## International and Cross-Functional Coordination

Because Meridian Software Inc. may process data across jurisdictions, the DPO works with Legal to determine transfer mechanisms and local compliance needs. Operational teams should not assume that a practice legal in one location is acceptable everywhere. Pacific Time deadlines in this policy are operational targets for internal teams; statutory deadlines in other jurisdictions still apply when they are stricter or differently measured.

## Employee Responsibilities

Every workforce member at Meridian Software Inc. must protect data according to classification, use only sanctioned systems for Confidential and Restricted data, never copy customer production data to personal devices or unsanctioned cloud tools, complete privacy training as required, and report suspected incidents quickly so the Security Team can be notified within four hours of discovery.

Employees should also avoid discussing Confidential or Restricted matters in public spaces, should lock screens when away from devices, and should verify recipients before sending sensitive exports. Mistakes should be reported promptly; early reporting is treated more constructively than concealment.

## Manager and Data Owner Responsibilities

Managers must ensure their teams understand classification levels—Public, Internal, Confidential, and Restricted—and that customer data defaults to Confidential unless a contract says Restricted. Data owners of Restricted datasets must ensure access is logged and must complete quarterly access reviews. Managers should allocate time for privacy training and for cooperation with data subject requests so acknowledgment within five business days and fulfillment within thirty calendar days where legally required remain achievable.

## Privacy Training

Privacy training is annual and is due within thirty (30) days of hire for new employees. People Operations and the DPO coordinate assignment and completion tracking, often through the HRIS or learning system. Employees who miss the annual cycle or the thirty-day new-hire due date may receive escalating reminders and may have access restrictions applied until training is completed.

Training covers classification, retention basics, approved tools, incident escalation, and how to route privacy requests. Completing training does not replace the obligation to follow this policy in daily work.

## Interaction with Onboarding and Employment Records

During onboarding, new hires receive accounts and may quickly gain access to Internal and Confidential systems. Managers and IT must ensure early access still follows least privilege. Personnel records created through hiring and employment are retained for seven years after termination under this policy. Probation status of ninety calendar days from start date does not reduce privacy or security obligations; new hires must protect data from Day 1 and complete privacy training within thirty days of hire.

## Secure Disposal and Media Handling

When data reaches the end of its retention period, or when devices are retired, disposal must prevent unauthorized recovery of Confidential and Restricted information. IT standards govern wiping, destruction, and certificate-of-destruction requirements for physical media. Paper records containing Confidential or Restricted data must be shredded using approved methods. Employees should not place sensitive printouts in ordinary recycling bins.

## Monitoring, Auditing, and Continuous Improvement

Meridian Software Inc. may audit compliance with this policy through technical controls, access review sampling, vendor assessments, and investigation findings. The DPO and CISO use audit results to improve training, tooling, and standards. Employees are expected to cooperate with privacy and security audits.

Metrics may include training completion rates, time to acknowledge privacy requests against the five-business-day target, time to fulfill against the thirty-calendar-day legal target where applicable, Restricted access review completion each quarter, and incident notification timeliness against the four-hour internal expectation.

## Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy require approval from the DPO and, where security controls are affected, the CISO. Exceptions must be documented, time-bounded, and accompanied by compensating controls when feasible. Informal verbal exceptions are not valid. Business urgency does not authorize copying customer production data to personal devices or unsanctioned cloud tools.

## Enforcement

Violations of this Data Privacy Policy may result in disciplinary action under Meridian Software Inc. conduct processes, up to and including termination, and may also require customer or regulatory notification depending on impact. Intentional circumvention of Restricted access logging, refusal to participate in quarterly access reviews by accountable data owners, or delayed breach escalation beyond four hours after discovery are treated as serious matters.

## Contact and Administration

The Data Protection Officer (DPO), working with the CISO, owns this policy and will publish updates as laws, products, and risks evolve. Privacy requests and classification questions should be routed through the published privacy intake channels at @meridian-software.example addresses. Security incidents and suspected breaches must be escalated so the Security Team is notified within four hours of discovery.

Meridian Software Inc. protects Public, Internal, Confidential, and Restricted data through clear ownership, least privilege, retention discipline—including seven years after contract end for customer data unless a shorter period is required by contract or law, and seven years after termination for employee personnel records—and timely response to privacy requests and incidents.
